# Governance Frameworks for 50/50 JV Co

> **When to read:** When discussing shareholder agreements, articles of association, decision rights, exit mechanisms, or dispute resolution.

---

## Shareholders' Agreement (SHA)

### Structure

1. Definitions and interpretation
2. Conditions precedent
3. Capitalisation and funding obligations
4. Corporate governance — board composition, quorum, chairman
5. Reserved matters — unanimous shareholder consent
6. Operating company governance — sovereign domain provisions
7. Management information and reporting rights
8. Restrictive covenants — non-compete, non-solicit, non-deal
9. Share transfer restrictions — pre-emption, lock-up
10. Exit mechanisms — deadlock, buy-sell, IPO, trade sale
11. Termination and consequences
12. Confidentiality
13. Dispute resolution escalation ladder
14. Governing law (English law) and jurisdiction

SHA prevails over Articles as between shareholders (express conflict clause). But cannot override Companies Act as against third parties. **JV Co must be a named party** to be contractually bound.

### Reserved Matters (Unanimous Consent)

**Constitutional:** Amend Articles/SHA, change name, create new share classes, reduce capital, apply for stock exchange admission.

**Financial:** Annual budget, expenditure above threshold, borrowing above threshold, granting security/guarantees, capital expenditure above threshold, declaring dividends, changing dividend policy.

**Corporate:** Acquisitions/disposals, forming subsidiaries, JV/partnership arrangements, mergers, winding-up, appointing/removing auditors.

**Contracts:** Contracts above threshold, related-party transactions (including with either operating company), contracts outside ordinary course, litigation settlements above threshold.

**People:** CEO/CFO appointment/removal, senior management remuneration changes, share option/incentive plan changes.

**IP:** Material IP licences or assignments.

**JV-Specific:** Changes to inter-company arrangements, decisions affecting operating company autonomy.

**Source:** Reflects BVCA model SHA practice and standard City firm drafting.

### Information Rights

- Monthly management accounts (within 15–20 business days of month end)
- Annual statutory accounts (audited if required)
- Annual business plan and budget (agreed 60 days before year end)
- KPI dashboard (monthly, including cross-company metrics)
- Material event notifications (litigation, regulatory, key customer loss)
- Board papers (5 business days before meetings)
- Audit rights for each founder (inspect books on reasonable notice)
- Operating company reporting to JV Co (revenue, costs recharged, material events)

## Articles of Association

### Model Articles Are Inadequate

Model Articles (SI 2008/3229) contain no director appointment rights, no share transfer restrictions, no reserved matters, and give chairman a casting vote. **Bespoke articles are essential.**

### Key Provisions

**Dual-class shares:** Class A (Founder A) and Class B (Founder B), each carrying director appointment rights. More robust than relying on SHA alone.

**Director appointments:** Each founder has the **right** (not merely ability) to appoint and remove their nominated director(s). Weighted appointment rights tied to share class.

**Quorum:** Board meetings require at least one director from each founder. Prevents either side constituting a rump board.

**Chairman:** Either rotate annually or explicitly **no casting vote**. A permanent casting vote creates de facto control — inconsistent with 50/50.

**Share transfers:**
- **Lock-up:** No transfers for 3–5 years (except to permitted transferees)
- **Pre-emption:** Right of first refusal at fair market value
- **Tag-along:** Non-selling founder can require inclusion in any third-party sale on same terms
- **Drag-along:** Founder with 100% buyer can compel other to sell on same terms

**Compulsory transfer on default:** Material SHA breach triggers forced sale at fair value (or discount for bad leaver).

### SHA/Articles Interaction (s.33 CA 2006)

Articles bind company and members as contract (s.33). SHA is private contract between shareholders. If director acts in breach of SHA but consistently with Articles, the act may still be valid as company law — remedy is contractual damages.

**Best practice:** Mirror reserved matters in both SHA and Articles. Ensures constitutional AND contractual effect.

## Sovereign Domain Model

> Each founder has absolute operational authority over their operating company. JV Co cannot override or interfere.

### Structural Implementation

- JV Co holds **no shares** in either operating company
- Operating companies wholly owned by each founder separately
- Economic relationship governed by inter-company agreements, not share ownership

### Contractual Implementation

- SHA contains express sovereign domain provisions
- Founder A: sole authority over product company (hiring, product, technology, pricing, capital)
- Founder B: sole authority over services company (equivalent)
- Neither founder's JV Co directors can instruct the other's operating company
- JV Co reserved matters do NOT extend to internal operating company management

### Protection Mechanisms

- Personal warranties from each founder re: operating company compliance
- Obligation to vote as operating company shareholder to ensure JV Co obligations performed
- SHA termination triggers if operating company persistently fails obligations

## Deadlock Resolution

### Architecture (Recommended)

**Operational deadlock** (budget, day-to-day):
1. Escalation to founders (15 days good faith negotiation)
2. CEDR mediation (30 days)
3. If unresolved → status quo preserved (neither party forces change)

**Fundamental deadlock** (irreconcilable strategic difference, sustained breakdown):
1. Mediation
2. Mandatory independent valuation establishes price floor
3. Buy-sell mechanism operates at no less than valuation floor

**Breach-caused deadlock:** Non-breaching founder has forced transfer right at fair value.

### Buy-Sell Mechanisms

**Shotgun clause:** Founder A names a price. Founder B must buy at that price or sell at that price. Triggering founder cannot withdraw.
- **Risk:** Financial asymmetry — wealthier founder can trigger at below-market knowing other can't fund purchase
- **Mitigation:** Mandatory independent valuation as floor price

**Russian roulette:** Iterative counter-offer mechanism. Similar asymmetry concerns.

Both are enforceable under English contract law. *Cable & Wireless v IBM* [2002] confirms mediation obligations enforceable as condition precedent to buy-sell activation.

## Exit Mechanisms

### Share Valuation

**Independent valuation (primary):** Big 4 or specialist firm, appointed by agreement or failing that by ICAEW President. Going-concern basis, willing buyer/seller. Agree in advance: no minority discount, no control premium, treatment of inter-company receivables, shareholder loans, goodwill.

**EBITDA formula (floor):** Pre-agreed multiple (4–8x trailing 12-month EBITDA). Simple but risks staleness.

**Recommended hybrid:** Higher of independent valuation and EBITDA formula. Prevents distressed-sale pricing.

### Good Leaver / Bad Leaver

**Good leaver events:** Death, permanent incapacity (6+ months), retirement, mutual agreement, SHA expiry.
**Consequence:** Full fair market value.

**Bad leaver events:** Material unremedied SHA breach, fraud/dishonesty, criminal conviction, insolvency, non-compete breach.
**Consequence:** Lower of cost and fair market value.

**Risk:** Bad leaver discount on 50% founder may trigger s.994 unfair prejudice petition (*O'Neill v Phillips*). Mitigate with LCIA arbitration clause covering all disputes including s.994 (*Fulham FC v Richards* [2011]).

### IP on Exit

- Operating company IP stays with owning founder (express warranty in SHA)
- JV Co-created IP: divide, revert to creator, or sell as JV Co asset
- Licences from operating companies to JV Co: terminate on exit unless continuing founder acquires JV Co
- Customer data: JV Co asset, subject to GDPR

### Post-Exit Non-Compete

- **Duration:** 12–24 months (enforceable for founders; beyond 24 months risky)
- **Scope:** Actual JV Co business, not whole industry. Carve out each operating company explicitly
- **Geography:** UK-wide generally enforceable if business is national
- Per *Tillman v Egon Zehnder* [2019]: blue pencil severance available if over-broad clause is clearly severable

## Dispute Resolution Escalation

| Step | Mechanism | Timeframe | Outcome |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Founders negotiate in good faith | 15 business days | Agreement or escalate |
| 2 | CEDR mediation | 30 business days | Settlement or escalate |
| 3a | Expert determination (valuation/technical) | 30 business days | Binding decision |
| 3b | LCIA arbitration (governance/other) | Per LCIA Rules | Binding award |

Each step is condition precedent to the next.

**CEDR:** Non-binding mediation, confidential, typically one day, significantly cheaper than litigation/arbitration.

**LCIA (Rules 2020):** Private, confidential, specialist arbitrators, enforceable in 168 countries (New York Convention). Limited appeal (Arbitration Act 1996 s.69). Can cover unfair prejudice claims (*Fulham FC v Richards*).

**Expert determination:** For specific technical/valuation questions. Binding except for manifest error or fraud (*Barclays Bank v Nylon Capital* [2011]).

**Court (when unavoidable):** Urgent injunctions (24–48 hours), unfair prejudice petitions if no arbitration clause, just and equitable winding-up as last resort (*Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries* [1973]).

## Practical Recommendations

1. **Engage separate solicitors** for negotiation phase, jointly instructed firm for final drafts. Budget: £15,000–£40,000 for this complexity.
2. **Mirror reserved matters** in both SHA and Articles.
3. **Build deadlock around mediation first**, buy-sell as last resort with mandatory valuation floor.
4. **Include LCIA arbitration clause** covering all disputes including s.994.
5. **Agree valuation methodology at outset** — single most common exit dispute source.
6. **Carve out operating companies** from non-competes explicitly.
7. **Document governance in an Operating Manual** (non-binding annex): escalation ladder, reporting calendar, KPI definitions, delegated authority matrices.
