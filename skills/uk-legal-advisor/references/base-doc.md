### The Four Structural Problems in Scenario 1

**Problem 1: The License Fee is Zero-Sum**

The license fee negotiation is the central economic relationship between the entities. But it's inherently adversarial:

- George wants it higher
- Brett wants it lower
- There's no "right" answer—just power dynamics and negotiation fatigue

This creates ongoing friction, even with good intentions. Every year, every new product, every pricing discussion reopens the wound.

---

**Problem 2: Employees Are Pitted Against Each Other**

Two separate profit pools means employees are economically misaligned:

- Novosapien employees benefit when license fees go UP
- Rebel Labs employees benefit when license fees go DOWN

This isn't hypothetical tension—it's structural. When people realize their bonus is inversely correlated with the other team's bonus, collaboration suffers. Information hoarding, blame-shifting, and "not my problem" attitudes emerge.

---

**Problem 3: Cost Savings Don't Flow Through**

When Novosapien reduces costs:

- Novosapien keeps 100% of the savings
- Rebel Labs gets nothing
- Clients get nothing (unless Novosapien voluntarily lowers fees)

When Rebel Labs reduces delivery costs:

- Rebel Labs keeps 100% of the savings
- Novosapien gets nothing
- The efficiency gains stay siloed

There's no mechanism for improvements in one entity to benefit the other. This kills the "one team" mentality.

---

**Problem 4: Defection Risk is Structurally Embedded**

The structure creates *latent* incentives for both parties to reduce dependency:

**Brett's defection paths:**

- Find alternative products (lower cost, better margins)
- Build products internally (own the whole value chain)
- Negotiate harder and harder on fees as leverage grows

**George's defection paths:**

- Find additional distribution channels (reduce dependency on Brett)
- Sell directly to clients (cut out the middleman)
- License to Rebel Labs' competitors

Neither party may *want* to defect today. But the structure makes defection *economically rational* under certain conditions. That's a ticking clock.

## Scenario 2: Three Entities with JV Co

### The Structure

`CLIENTS
    ↓
JV CO (George and Brett)
    │
    ├── reimburses at cost ──→ NOVOSAPIEN (100% George)
    │
    └── reimburses at cost ──→ REBEL LABS (100% Brett)`

### The P&L Flow

`JV CO
Revenue: Client payments                     £100,000
- Novosapien cost reimbursement               £30,000
- Rebel Labs cost reimbursement               £35,000
= JV Co Profit                                £35,000

Distribution:
- Employee profit share pool (30%)            £10,500
- George (35%)                                £12,250
- Brett (35%)                                 £12,250`

### The Founder Compensation Question

You raised an important point: *"Both Brett and George want to pay their families."*

Here's how this works in the JV Co model:

**Option A: Founder Salaries from JV Co**

George and Brett each receive a salary directly from JV Co, as officers/directors of the commercial entity. This is:

- A fixed, agreed amount
- Treated as a JV Co cost (comes out before profit calculation)
- Subject to whatever agreement you make with each other

**Example:**

`JV CO P&L
Revenue: Client payments                     £100,000
- Novosapien cost reimbursement               £30,000
- Rebel Labs cost reimbursement               £35,000
- George salary (from JV Co)                   £5,000
- Brett salary (from JV Co)                    £5,000
= JV Co Profit                                £25,000

Distribution:
- Employee profit share pool (30%)             £7,500
- George (35%)                                 £8,750
- Brett (35%)                                  £8,750`

**George's total take:** £5,000 (salary) + £8,750 (profit share) = £13,750
**Brett's total take:** £5,000 (salary) + £8,750 (profit share) = £13,750

**The principle:** Founder salaries are a *cost* to JV Co, taken before profit distribution. This ensures both founders can pay their families a predictable amount, regardless of profit fluctuations.

## Governance: Why This Structure Protects Both Founders

### The Context: Both Founders Have Been Burned

You and Brett aren't naive first-time founders looking for a co-founder because it sounds fun. You've both been through partnerships that went wrong. You've experienced:

- Decisions made without your input that affected your livelihood
- Equity disputes when things got valuable
- The slow erosion of trust when incentives diverged
- The painful, expensive process of unwinding something built on unclear foundations

That history matters. It's why you've been reluctant to merge into a single company. It's why "co-founder" feels risky even though you're operating like co-founders.

**The JV Co structure is designed to give you the benefits of partnership while preserving the protections you need.**

---

### The Core Principle: Sovereign Domains with Shared Outcomes

Here's the fundamental governance logic:

`┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                 │
│   GEORGE has absolute authority over NOVOSAPIEN                 │
│   - Product strategy                                            │
│   - Technical architecture                                      │
│   - Engineering hiring and management                           │
│   - IP development                                              │
│   - How the product evolves                                     │
│                                                                 │
│   No one—not Brett, not JV Co—can override these decisions.     │
│                                                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                 │
│   BRETT has absolute authority over REBEL LABS                  │
│   - Service delivery methodology                                │
│   - Client relationship management                              │
│   - Lab operations and staffing                                 │
│   - Delivery quality standards                                  │
│   - How the service is executed                                 │
│                                                                 │
│   No one—not George, not JV Co—can override these decisions.    │
│                                                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                 │
│   GEORGE AND BRETT jointly govern JV CO                         │
│   - Commercial strategy                                         │
│   - Pricing                                                     │
│   - Client contracts                                            │
│   - Channel partnerships                                        │
│   - Profit distribution                                         │
│                                                                 │
│                                                                 │
│   Major decisions require mutual consent.                       │
│                                                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘`

**What this means in practice:**

George can wake up tomorrow and decide to rebuild the entire Novosapien product architecture. Brett can't stop him. But because George's income comes from JV Co profit, he's only going to make that decision if he believes it serves the shared outcome.

Brett can wake up tomorrow and decide to restructure all three Labs. George can't stop him. But because Brett's income comes from JV Co profit, he's only going to make that decision if he believes it serves the shared outcome.

**The structure gives you veto power in your domain, but the economics ensure you use that power wisely.**

---

### Why This Solves the "Burned Before" Problem

| Past Partnership Problem | How JV Co Structure Solves It |
| --- | --- |
| **"My partner made decisions that affected me without consulting me"** | Your domain is sovereign. No one can make product decisions for George. No one can make service decisions for Brett. |
| **"We disagreed on direction and there was no tiebreaker"** | Domains are clearly separated. Product = George's call. Service = Brett's call. Only truly shared decisions require consensus. |
| **"My partner extracted value at my expense"** | All profit flows to JV Co. Neither party can extract value from their entity at the other's expense—there's no profit in the individual entities to extract. |
| **"When things got valuable, the equity split became contentious"** | Ownership is locked: 100% of your entity, part of JV Co. No ambiguity, no renegotiation. |
| **"I couldn't exit cleanly when the relationship soured"** | Clear separation of assets. If you part ways, you keep your entity and its IP. JV Co can be wound down or bought out with predefined mechanics. |
